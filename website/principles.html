Principles

Seven commitments, no exceptions.

Every design decision in Neyam Vaani must satisfy all seven of these. They are codified in the project's Tender UX Principles and Privacy Charter, and no contribution — however clever — may weaken them.

  1. Offline-first

    Every core feature works with zero internet. Connectivity is an upgrade, never a requirement. A user in a village with a 2G bar loses nothing.

  2. Cheap-hardware-first

    The reference device is a $200 Android phone with 2GB of RAM — a Redmi 9A, not a flagship. Cold start under three seconds; app download under 80MB.

  3. Voice-first

    Speech in, speech out. Typing remains available as a fallback for people who want it, never as a requirement for anyone.

  4. Vernacular-first

    Tamil, Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Telugu, Marathi, Bhojpuri and Maithili at parity — deliberately including the low-resource languages commercial assistants skip. English is a secondary language, never the default.

  5. Private by default

    All intelligence runs on the device. No cloud, no accounts, no telemetry, no advertising. Voice recordings exist only in memory and are never stored. The full charter is on the Privacy page.

  6. Tender UX

    No dark patterns, no engagement hooks, no unrequested notifications, no streaks or badges. Errors are worded kindly. Loading states are honest. Attention is treated as the user's property, not the app's.

  7. Grandmother-usable

    Usable unaided by someone who has never been taught computers — the standard every release is tested against, not a stretch goal.

The Tender UX test

Before anything ships, it must respect seven things: the user's attention, intelligence, time, language, data, autonomy and dignity. If any answer is no, the work goes back.

If a seventy-year-old in a Tamil village cannot use it without help, it is broken.

In practice this bans a surprising amount of ordinary software: the notification nobody asked for, the fake "thinking" spinner, the celebration screen, the usage streak, the error code where a kind sentence should be. The complete rules live in TENDER_UX_PRINCIPLES.md.

The voice personality

When Vaani speaks, it is calm, patient and slightly slower than default, with brief pauses between sentences. It uses common words, answers in one to three sentences, and says "I don't know" rather than guessing. It is a tool, not a friend — there is deliberately no persona, no backstory, no manufactured intimacy.
