A voice assistant that speaks your language — offline, on any phone.

Neyam Vaani is a free, open source AI assistant for Android that runs entirely on the device — including the speech recognition, the language model, and the voice. It is built for people who speak Tamil, Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Telugu, Marathi, Bhojpuri or Maithili, and for phones the industry has stopped optimizing for.

Overview

Hundreds of millions of people in India own a smartphone but cannot comfortably use it, because the phone demands typing in English, a constant internet connection, and hardware that keeps getting more expensive. Vaani starts from the opposite assumptions. You speak to it in your own language; it answers in your own language; and everything it needs to do so fits on the phone in your pocket.

In practice, Vaani answers spoken questions, drafts and sends WhatsApp and SMS messages, reads letters and medicine labels aloud, translates between the languages it speaks, fills government forms by voice, sets reminders, and carries out ordinary phone actions — calls, torch, clipboard — without a single keystroke.

If a seventy-year-old in a Tamil village cannot use it without help, it is broken. This is the test every feature must pass.

Principles

Seven commitments govern every design decision. They are codified in the project's Tender UX Principles and Privacy Charter, and no contribution may weaken them.

  1. Offline-first

    Every core feature works with zero internet. Connectivity is an upgrade, never a requirement.

  2. Cheap-hardware-first

    The reference device is a $200 Android phone with 2GB of RAM, with a cold start under three seconds.

  3. Voice-first

    Speech in, speech out. Typing remains available as a fallback, never as a requirement.

  4. Vernacular-first

    Tamil, Hindi, Bengali, Kannada, Telugu, Marathi, Bhojpuri and Maithili at parity — including the languages commercial assistants skip.

  5. Private by default

    All intelligence runs on the device. No cloud, no accounts, no telemetry, no advertising. Recordings are never stored.

  6. Tender UX

    No dark patterns, no engagement hooks, no unrequested notifications. Errors are worded kindly; attention is respected.

  7. Grandmother-usable

    Usable unaided by someone who has never been taught computers — the standard, not a stretch goal.

Technology

Vaani assembles proven open components into a pipeline that fits inside a low-end phone. Each stage runs on the device.

  1. Speech recognition

    Whisper (via whisper.cpp) transcribes speech and detects which of the nine languages is being spoken, with Silero detecting when you have finished.

  2. Understanding

    A fast on-device classifier routes the request; Qwen 2.5 — a multilingual model small enough for a phone, running via llama.cpp — handles questions, drafting and explanation. No server is involved at any point.

  3. Knowledge and action

    Factual questions draw on a local knowledge base with hybrid full-text and semantic search. Requests that do something — send, call, remind — are carried out through standard Android actions, with your confirmation.

  4. Speech output

    Piper neural voices speak the reply in your language, with a slower, calm default pace and instant interruption when you begin talking again.

The full architecture, model budget and stack rationale are documented in docs/architecture.md.

Questions

Does it really work without internet?

Yes — 100% of core features are offline. Speech recognition, the language model, translation and text-to-speech all run on the phone. The only network use is optional, opt-in language pack downloads.

What does it cost?

Nothing, ever. Apache-2.0 open source. No ads, no accounts, no premium tier — these are permanent product decisions written into the project charter.

Which phones are supported?

Android phones with as little as 2GB RAM (e.g., Redmi 9A). The app auto-selects smaller AI models on low-RAM devices and keeps its download under 80MB.

What happens to my voice data?

Voice exists in memory only and is never stored. Conversation text is encrypted on-device (SQLCipher) and deletable at any time. Nothing is ever uploaded — there is no server to upload to.

Who is building this?

Neyam OpenSc, an open source organization — with native-speaker language stewards owning quality for each language.

When can I try it?

The project is in Phase 0 of a 90-day roadmap to public beta. Follow the repository for releases.

Contribute

The code, documentation, prompts and roadmap are public from day one. The most valuable contribution is a native speaker's ear — as a language steward you own the quality of Vaani in your language.
